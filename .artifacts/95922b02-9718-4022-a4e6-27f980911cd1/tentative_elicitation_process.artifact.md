# AquaWatch: Tentative Elicitation Process

This document outlines the systematic approach to gathering requirements from stakeholders to ensure the platform meets both scientific and community needs.

## 1. Stakeholder Analysis
*   **Identify**: Categorize stakeholders into Contributors (Citizens), Verifiers (Authorities), and Analysts (Researchers).
*   **Profile**: Map their motivations, pain points, and technical literacy using the Value Proposition Canvas.

## 2. Elicitation Methods
*   **Interviews**: One-on-one sessions with environmental officials to define verification workflows and data standards.
*   **Surveys**: Distributed to community groups and students to identify the most critical water quality concerns and desired app features.
*   **Workshops**: Collaborative "Co-Creation" sessions with both citizens and scientists to define the "Recording" workflow.
*   **Brainstorming**: Generating ideas for gamification and engagement strategies to sustain long-term participation.

## 3. Scoping & Co-Creation
*   **Research Question**: Defining exactly what data is needed (pH, TDS, etc.) and what accuracy is acceptable for "Estimation" vs. "Professional" levels.
*   **Role Definition**: Determining if users are simple contributors or if they will also help analyze data.
*   **Feature Prioritization**: Ranking features based on impact and technical feasibility (e.g., AI Analysis vs. Basic Reporting).

## 4. Iterative Prototyping
*   **Low-Fi Mockups**: Early feedback on UI/UX to ensure the app is intuitive for non-technical users.
*   **Pilot Testing**: Deploying a beta version to a small community group to observe real-world data collection and identify friction points.

## 5. Validation & Documentation
*   **Requirement Specification**: Translating findings into a formal Functional and Non-Functional Requirements Document.
*   **Stakeholder Sign-off**: Ensuring agreement on the final scope before moving into full-scale development.
